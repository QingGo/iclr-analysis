# ICLR 2025/2026 Paper Comparative Analysis

This project targets the ICLR 2025 and ICLR 2026 paper collections and uses an LLM to extract term frequencies from the papers, identifying annual hot topics and optimization directions. Statistics and visualization are automatically generated by the project’s code.

## Quick Start
``` python
# Set required environment variables in .env; see: .env_example
YEAR=2025 uv run main.py pipeline --extract-max-concurrency=50 --crawl-max-concurrency=10
YEAR=2026 uv run main.py pipeline --extract-max-concurrency=50 --crawl-max-concurrency=10
```
For detailed usage, see: [Usage Guide](./USAGE_EN.md). For the project’s technical design, see: [Design Doc](./DESIGN_DOC_EN.md)

## Conclusions and Recommendations
Summary:
- Research focus is shifting from “single model/algorithm scaling” toward “evaluation ecosystems, reasoning capability, and training process optimization”; efficiency remains a long-term main theme.
- Reinforcement learning topics rise markedly in the 2026 collection (submitted in the second half of 2025), likely linked to increased demand for planning/interaction in complex tasks.
- The importance of evaluation and benchmarks continues to increase, reflecting stronger community attention to comparability, reproducibility, and robustness.
- Recommendations:
  - In method design, balance efficiency and pipeline (e.g., two-stage training, unified frameworks, iterative refinement), and strengthen evaluation on benchmarks and zero-shot generalization.
  - The “reasoning + reinforcement learning” direction for LLMs merits further investment; focus on the engineering feasibility of sampling and large-scale data processing.

## Methodology
- Data scope: Titles and abstracts of ICLR 2025 and ICLR 2026 papers (statistics by term occurrence).
- Annual sample notes:
  - 2025: Uses titles and abstracts of officially accepted papers; total 3704.
  - 2026: Not yet officially accepted; selects submissions with average review score ≥ 6 (average across all reviews for each submission) as the statistical sample; total 1798.
- Statistics: Top-20 keyword frequencies, Top-20 optimization directions, word clouds, and share analysis.
- Processing pipeline:
  - Use an LLM to extract keywords from each paper’s title and abstract, output a normalized term list; Prompt:
    ```
    'You are a seasoned academic assistant. Based on the paper title, abstract, and body snippets, return only a JSON object: {"tags": [...], "optimizations": [...]}.'
    "- tags: no more than 20, all are keywords or short phrases;"
    '- optimizations: no more than 10, must be the paper’s optimization directions or methods compared to prior work; focus on improvements in methods, strategies, architectures, or data pipelines; each item is a 2–3 keyword phrase, avoid full sentences and punctuation, e.g., "data augmentation", "multi-task training", "adapter tuning".'
    "- Output pure JSON, without explanations or other text."
    ```
  - Aggregate term frequencies, compute the Top-20, and generate visualizations. In charts, the x-axis is occurrence `count`, and the y-axis is the corresponding term label.

## Year-over-Year Comparison and Analysis

- Corpus size difference: Top-20 total counts (keywords) are ~5851 in 2025 and ~2354 in 2026; (optimizations) ~751 in 2025 and ~309 in 2026. The drop in absolute values shows that direct year-over-year comparison is affected by sample size; proportions and rankings are more informative. Additionally, the 2026 sample uses submissions with average review score ≥ 6, so sample size and distribution may differ from the officially accepted set.
- Topic share changes (keywords):
  - Reinforcement learning share rises from ~7.0% to ~14.1%, indicating a substantial increase in relative attention.
  - Benchmark share rises from ~4.8% to ~7.4%, with evaluation systems and measurement orientation becoming more prominent.
  - Large language models share slightly drops from ~11.5% to ~10.4%, still core but less singular.
  - Diffusion models share decreases from ~7.3% to ~6.4%, attention slightly recedes.
- Optimization direction changes:
  - `computational efficiency` ranks first in both years, indicating efficiency optimization remains the mainline; the drop in absolute counts signals sample-size impact.
  - 2026 places more emphasis on pipeline and system-level improvements, such as `unified framework`, `two-stage training`, `iterative refinement`, `scalable training`, and `multimodal integration`, reflecting a shift from point optimizations to holistic pipeline engineering and scalability.
  - Traditional model-level optimizations like `parameter efficiency` decline in share, while `data efficiency` and large-scale data/sampling-related optimizations rise in importance.
